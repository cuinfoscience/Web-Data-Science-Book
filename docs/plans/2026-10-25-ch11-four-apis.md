## 3. The four APIs, and the idea each one carries

| API | Headline | What the reader does | Key (environment variable) | Case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Census** (ACS 5-year) | What can I ask for? | Read `variables.json` and `geography.json` to find a variable and a place's code; reshape the list-of-lists response; meet the `-666666666` sentinel | `CENSUS_KEY` | Boulder County against Colorado's other counties |
| **FRED** | Let the server compute | `units`, `frequency` and `aggregation_method` turn one series into percent changes or annual averages, without pandas | `FRED_KEY` | Denver-area CPI against the nation |
| **EIA API v2** | Let the server filter | A route describes its children and its facets; `facets[stateid][]=CO`, `data[]`, `frequency` and `sort` narrow the data; `offset` and `length` page it, against chapter 10's `continue` | `EIA_KEY` | Colorado's electricity prices and generation mix |
| **Congress.gov API v3** | Follow the links | A bill's record links to its actions, cosponsors and text; `next` links page a list; `fromDateTime` asks what changed; `X-RateLimit-Remaining` reports what's left | `DATAGOV_KEY` | Bills sponsored by Colorado's delegation, and how far each got |

The chapter's ending joins two of them on Colorado's identifiers: Census's FIPS code `08` against EIA's `CO`. That keeps "Combining Data" while teaching that the real work of a join is matching identifiers.
