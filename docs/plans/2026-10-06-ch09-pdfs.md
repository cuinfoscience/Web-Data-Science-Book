### 3.3 pypdf and pdfplumber on the same pages

- **Minutes, page 1.** pypdf splits words: "A dams", "CIT / Y COUNCIL", "Ma / yor", "Counc / il". pdfplumber, which groups characters by position, gives "Adams", "CITY COUNCIL", "Mayor", and "Council". The file has no space character inside "Adams", and its glyphs touch (the A ends at x = 328.22 and the d starts at 328.20). So the space is pypdf's reconstruction, not something in the file.
- **Revenue summary (December 2024, page 2), pypdf, plain mode.** Each data row comes out on one line. One regex recovers 18 rows. A label that wraps loses its first line: "Total Sales & Use Tax based on / current economic activity" comes out as "current economic activity".
- **The same page, pypdf, layout mode.** The columns stay in place, but `643,062` becomes `6 43 ,06 2`, and words split ("r ental", "20 24 unauditedtax es").
- **The same page, pdfplumber `extract_tables()`.** The default strategy follows ruling lines.
  - It finds 4 tables: 2 real ones and 2 fragments.
  - The header is three rows deep, with `None` in the merged cells.
  - **The "Total Sales and Use Tax" row is missing.** It has a rule under it but no vertical rules, so no cells form there.
  - On Exhibit 1 it finds 9 tables, 3 of them real, and the totals row comes back almost empty.
- **The fix.** Pass the table's own column edges as `explicit_vertical_lines` for a crop of the table, and the totals row comes back.
- **2018's Exhibit 3, which has no rules.** The lines strategy finds nothing. The text strategy recovers the data rows cleanly, and splits the header words ("Sale", "s Tax").

### 3.4 Checks that caught real problems

- **Sums.** The five components of "Total Sales & Use Tax based on current economic activity" (December 2024) add up to 171,006,200, and the report prints 171,006,199. That is rounding, so a sum check needs a tolerance.
- **Row counts.** The regex that found 18 rows in December 2024 found 12 in December 2023, which writes negatives in parentheses: `(319,979)`, `(18.75%)`. No error was raised.
- **Agreement between documents.** The 2023 figures that December 2023 and December 2024 both print agree, 12 of 12. No restatement turned up in this pair.
- **Metadata.** Three reports from 2018, 2021, and 2024 all say "March 2018", inherited from a template. Don't date a document by its metadata.
- **The chapter's attendance regex on real minutes.** It returned 4 of 9 names, through both pypdf and pdfplumber.

### 3.5 OCR on the image-only Exhibit 3 (December 2024)

- **The run.** `pytesseract.image_to_string(image, config="--psm 6")`, on the embedded image in grayscale (2545×1331 pixels), took 3.6 s.
- **The output.** It produced 30 lines that begin with a year: five categories × 2019–2024.
  - One regex parsed 28 of them.
  - The other two had a stray "—" that the OCR read from a table rule.
- **The checks.**
  - **Arithmetic.** In all 28 rows, the twelve months add up to the row's total within $2, which is rounding. A misread digit would miss by thousands.
  - **The text layer.** The OCR'd 2024 totals for the four categories and the grand total equal the same report's text layer (Exhibit 1 and the summary page): 142,513,015; 10,498,546; 14,930,902; 5,965,968; 173,908,431.
- **Errors seen.**
  - Commas read as periods (`8.832.480`).
  - A dropped decimal point (`-747%` for −7.47%).
  - "oct" for "OCT".
  - Upscaling 2× added noise (`~—-:15, 102,815`).
- **Confidence scores are a weak alarm.** 163 of 722 words scored below 80, yet every parsed row passed the arithmetic check.

**9.2 What's Inside a PDF.**
- **What a page is.** A content stream of drawing instructions. Glyphs from fonts are placed at coordinates, each with a map back to Unicode, and that map can be missing. Lines, rectangles, and images sit beside them.
- **What it doesn't store.** No words, lines, paragraphs, tables, or reading order: all of those are inferred.
- **Cells.**
  - The first few `page.chars` of the minutes: `text`, `x0`, `top`, `fontname`, `size`.
  - Counts of characters, rectangles, lines, and images on the revenue summary page.
- **Tagged PDFs.** A structure tree that screen readers follow. It ties to accessibility (9.1, and the Social History).
- **Metadata,** and the stale "March 2018".
- **Figure.** pdfplumber's debug render of the summary page: characters boxed, rules drawn.

**9.8 Checking an Extraction.** This is the chapter's validation and auditing section. It comes before both case studies, and both apply it.
- **Why.** Extraction fails silently: a missing row raises no error (§3.3, §3.4).
- **The checks, cheapest first,** each with its Boulder example:
  1. **Look at the page beside the output.** The debug image.
  2. **Count.** Rows and fields you expect: 18 against 12, and nine council seats.
  3. **Types and ranges.** Numbers parse, and percentages are plausible (`-747%`).
  4. **Sums.** Components against totals, within a tolerance: 171,006,200 against 171,006,199.
  5. **Two tools agree.** The pypdf regex against the pdfplumber table.
  6. **Two documents agree.** 2023's figures in two December reports; OCR against Exhibit 1.
  7. **A sample coded by hand.** Precision and recall for text fields such as attendance.
  8. **Provenance.** The manifest, file hashes, library versions, and dated outputs, so someone else can audit and redo the work.
- **Write the checks as a function** that returns a list of problems, and never drop a row in silence.
- **An audit of your extraction is not a financial audit.** The reports themselves say "unaudited".
- **Datasheets** (@gebru2021datasheets) for writing down what failed.

### 8.2 Records and accessibility law

**How this was checked.** Each fact was read on 2026-10-06 from the Federal Register, the CFR, ada.gov, W3C, Colorado's signed acts and statutes, the rules at 8 CCR 1501-11 (Cornell's copy; the Secretary of State's copy returned errors), and the city's own pages. The parts marked "reading" are interpretations, not settled law.

- **Federal: the ADA Title II web rule.**
  - **The rule.** [89 FR 31320](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/04/24/2024-07758/nondiscrimination-on-the-basis-of-disability-accessibility-of-web-information-and-services-of-state) (April 24, 2024), at 28 CFR 35.200–35.205. It adopts WCAG 2.1 Level AA for state and local governments' web content.
  - **The deadline moved.** An interim final rule, [91 FR 20902](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-20/html/2026-07663.htm) (April 20, 2026), moved the deadline for entities of 50,000 people or more from April 24, 2026 to **April 26, 2027**. The rule says entities still have "an ongoing obligation" under Title II meanwhile.
  - **The challenge.** The National Federation of the Blind sued in May 2026 to undo the delay ([NFB](https://nfb.org/about-us/press-room/national-federation-blind-sues-government-over-delay-accessibility-rules); [Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/customer-experience/2026/05/disability-advocates-sue-over-website-accessibility-delays/413781/)). The case number and docket weren't verified.
  - **Boulder is in the large group.** Its 2020 Census count is 108,250. That figure was seen through search results only, because census.gov refused the request.
  - **Two exceptions matter for PDFs** ([28 CFR 35.201](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title28-vol1/xml/CFR-2025-title28-vol1-sec35-201.xml)):
    - archived web content, which must meet four conditions;
    - "preexisting conventional electronic documents", which means PDFs and similar files posted before the entity's compliance date, "unless such documents are currently used to apply for, gain access to, or participate in" its services.
    - Reading: the 2018–2024 Revenue Report PDFs likely fall under the second exception.
    - [ada.gov](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/) adds that people with disabilities can still request an accessible format, and that council minutes created after the compliance date don't fall under the exception.
  - **Three WCAG 2.1 criteria describe an image of a table** ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/2018/REC-WCAG21-20180605/)):
    - 1.1.1, non-text content needs a text alternative;
    - 1.3.1, structure must be "programmatically determined";
    - 1.4.5, "text is used to convey information rather than images of text."
- **Colorado: HB21-1110, C.R.S. 24-34-802.**
  - **Coverage and deadline.** It applies to "any state or local government", and its deadline was **July 1, 2024**.
  - **HB24-1454.** It gave entities that showed good-faith effort immunity until July 1, 2025. That immunity has expired.
  - **Remedies.** A court order, plus actual damages or $3,500 per plaintiff per violation.
  - **Standards.** 8 CCR 1501-11 adopts WCAG 2.1 A and AA. An entity can comply in one of several ways, among them "providing reasonable modifications or accommodations, when requested", with a posted accessibility statement.
  - Reading: a "contact us for a screen readable version" note may fit Colorado's framework. It is a weaker fit for the federal rule after April 2027, which allows a separate accessible version only when direct accessibility isn't possible.
- **The Colorado Open Records Act gives a format right.** C.R.S. 24-72-203(3.5), added by [SB17-040](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2017a_040_signed.pdf):
  - **Format.** A record kept in a sortable format must be provided in a sortable format, and a searchable one in a searchable format. A custodian "shall not convert a digital public record into a non-searchable format before transmission."
  - **Timing.** Three working days, extendable by up to seven.
  - **Fees.** The first hour of research is free; after that, at most $41.37 an hour ([Legislative Council](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/cora_fee_change_memo_june_2024_accessible.pdf)). Emailed digital records carry no fee.
  - **Boulder's practice.** Boulder's [Central Records](https://bouldercolorado.gov/services/central-records) emails records, and its request form lets you ask for accessible ones.
  - Reading: if the exhibits come from a spreadsheet, a request could ask for that spreadsheet.
- **For the chapter.**
  - Present these facts, with dates and sources, as context for soft enclosure and accessibility.
  - Don't say the city is breaking the law. That is a legal conclusion, the exceptions likely apply, and the dates are moving.
  - One irony is worth a sentence: Colorado's signed acts for these laws are untagged scans. OCR turns their strike-through text into strings like "has-the-same-meaning-arset-forthirr-Tit-Fe-II-of", a ready example for 9.7.
